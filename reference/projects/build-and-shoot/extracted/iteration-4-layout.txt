      The player will choose their color set for their Avatar, Level
Blocks, and Territory Marks. Once the color sets are decided, each

Progression Procedures:

1. Players take their turns in the previously determined order. The active player
can use an Action Card to perform an Action. Players can use an unlimited
number of  Action Cards per turn, performing as many actions as they have
cards.

     The Actions that players can perform by using an Action Card:

    •  Moving (Further explain in Movement System)

    •  Shooting (Further explain in Combat System)

    •  Building (Build a block by using a Resource Card)

    •  Use Items and Place Units(Further explained in Transitional Mechanics)

2. After a player completes their last Action and decides not to use any more
Action Cards, they enter the Settlement and AI Activity phase.

     Settlement:

    •  The player checks the surrounding nine grid spaces for any items to
       collect. If  items are found, they are automatically picked up and added to
       the player ’s inventory.

     AI Activity:

    •  All surviving AI Units on the map will perform one turn of  actions based on
       their behavior logic; a detailed explanation is in the AI Systems section.

3. After the Settlement and AI Activity phase, Players can use their Resource
Cards to purchase any items; the detailed explanation of  the cost and gains of
purchase is listed in the Special Procedures section. However, they are not
allowed to use any items during this step.

4. After the player ’s purchasing phase, their turn concludes, and the next player ’s
turn begins.

Combat System:

Player Avatar:

     The combat system is most dependent on the player's position. When the
player performs a shooting action, the range of  a virtual bullet (since there are
no actual bullets in the game's components, the player needs to visualize and
compute its trajectory) has a set of  calculating logic that determines how far it
will ﬂy and what target it will hit. The starting position of  the bullet is the location
of  the player ’s avatar.

First, similar to the movement system, the bullet ’s range is calculated by moving
it from one grid to another, with each movement covering 1 unit of  grid space.
However, unlike avatar movement, the bullet can travel diagonally, vertically, and
horizontally.

The player will roll a D6 to determine their base shooting range. For example, if
the player rolls a six, the bullet can move six units in any direction on the grid (in
a straight line without turning directions). If  the bullet moves over the player ’s
own Territory Marks, its movement range extends by one additional unit (this
effect can be triggered repeatedly).

     Example: The player plays an Ammo Card during their Action turn to
attempt a shot and rolls a 4.

     Territory Marks are the regular range of  the bullet without the inﬂuence of
Territory Marks. In contrast, the graph on the right illustrates how the bullet ’s
maximum range is extended when passing through Territory Marks in one
direction(right side). The original range of  four units is expanded to seven units,
as three Territory Marks increased the range by three additional units.

     This design aims to balance the inﬂuence of  randomness elements in the
game, as the outcome of  D6 will determine the range of  the shooting Action. The
extension mechanic strategically emphasizes both the move and building
actions.

     Players standing at a higher level can shoot at players at a lower level. A
player's level depends on how many blocks they are standing on. Players can
build up to 3 block levels in the game. When a player at a lower position shoots
at a player positioned higher, the bullet will destroy one block at that gird.

     This design is intended to give strategic players an advantage by allowing
them to plan their positioning carefully. Players at higher levels can ignore lower-
level obstacles, providing them with a tactical beneﬁt.

=== page 17 ===
     This inventory system design aims to balance the potential beneﬁts of
different cards; I assign Weights and stackable properties to each type. For
example, HP Cards and Action Cards are essential resources—HP prevents game
failure, and Action Cards are required for basic actions like Moving and gaining
resources. So, I designed these cards to be stackable but with a low stacking limit.
This ensures players can hold a minimum amount of  these cards but must
consume additional weight units to carry more.

This design forces players to strategize their limited hand capacity, deciding
between holding more HP/Action Cards for survival and utility or allocating
weight to other cards for broader tactical options.

Speciﬁcations (Attack Guard):

• Idle: Remain stationary in place.

• Patrol: Move in a loop along a route planned by the player. The patrol path
  must be continuous, connecting start and endpoints. No maximum or minium
  length of  route. When this state is active, the avatar moves forward 2 grid units
  per turn.

• Search: Act three times in total, moving forward 3 grid units each time while
  attempting to locate the player.

• Attack: Inﬂict damage once on a player within attack range, with the damage
  value based on the level of  Attack Guard.

• Chase: After discovering an enemy player, advance towards them, moving 2
  grid units at a time.

• Restore: If  the unit is in this state, it recovers 1 HP per turn.

• Flee: Move in the opposite direction from the nearest enemy player,
  advancing 3 grid units at a time.


Attack Guards can be upgraded in this game system, with each upgrade costing
twice as much as the previous one. Upgrading from Level 1 to Level 2 costs 10
Territory Marks, starting with an initial cost of  5 Territory Marks for Level 1.

Once a player upgrades to a certain level for the ﬁrst time, they unlock the ability
to purchase that level of  Attack Guard during the Purchase Phase. For example, if
a player hasn't upgraded to Level 2, they can't buy it for 10 Territory Marks.
However, if  they've reached Level 2 or higher before, they can purchase Level 2,
regardless of  current game ownership.

The main purpose of  the initial upgrade is to unlock the right to buy higher-level
Units, not just to upgrade directly.
