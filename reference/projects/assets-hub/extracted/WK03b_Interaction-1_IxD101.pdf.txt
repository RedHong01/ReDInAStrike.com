Direct vs. Indirect
Competition
=== page 11 ===
Direct Competition
“Direct competitors are companies that offer the same or a very 
similar value proposition to your current or future customers.”
Levy, Jaime. UX Strategy (p. 79).
=== page 12 ===
Indirect Competition
“Indirect competitors offer a different value proposition, but somehow 
their solution may satisfy the needs of your target customer.”
Levy, Jaime. UX Strategy (p. 80).
=== page 13 ===
What is a Competitive Analysis?
An assessment of the strengths and 
weaknesses of current and 
potential competitors.
Identiﬁes opportunities, standards, 
and threats in the market. 
=== page 14 ===
Why do a Competitive Analysis?
Research and Competitive Analysis can help answer: 
● What else is out there? 
● What is working? What isn’t? 
● What features do competitors have that your users will expect? 
● What features are missing in the marketplace?
=== page 15 ===
Conducting a Competitive Analysis
1. Deﬁne  your purpose statement / objectives.
2. Select  your competitors.
3. Determine  your assessment criteria.
4. Collect  your research, resources, & materials.
5. Analyze  and organize your research.
6. Present  your ﬁndings and takeaways.

HW3: (group) Feature Prioritization 
KEY OBJECTIVE(S)
● List and prioritize your feature ideas gathered from previous ideation and research such as 
interviews and competitive analysis.
ASSIGNMENT
1. Pick a tool to capture and organize feature ideas from your group (Figjam, Miro, etc.). Consider 
afﬁnity mapping to collect ideas and group by theme or feature.
2. Prioritize what is important to include in your project. Consider the following when prioritizing:
○ What is the feature and how does it function? (what are the interactions, dependencies, etc.)
○ How might it be used?
○ Why is this important?
○ Where speciﬁcally might it ﬁt into the ﬂow of your platform/service/app etc.?

What is a Scenario? 
● Scenarios are  goal-driven interactions
between a user and a system. 
● Scenarios help you  understand the 
various contexts  in which your users 
will be using these tools.

=== page 31 ===
Components of a Good Scenario 
Scenarios are usually centered around one main task that is key to your 
product/service/platform and includes 5 elements:
1. Your persona (Actor)
2. A motivator
3. An intention or intent
4. An action
5. A resolution

What is a User Flow?
Visualization of a series of steps a user takes to achieve a goal
How To Create A User Flow: A Step-By-Step Guide
=== page 35 ===
Why Create User Flows?
● Increase awareness of the  user 
and goals
● Helps focus on the user’s  tasks
● Sets a foundation for future 
design decisions
● Supports  troubleshooting
● Improves team  communication
(internal and external)
=== page 36 ===
Steps to Create User Flows
● Gather preliminary information
● Create a title for the user ﬂow
● Sketch each step
● Keep it simple
● Include explanation where helpful (annotations or text labels)
● Evaluate and iterate
